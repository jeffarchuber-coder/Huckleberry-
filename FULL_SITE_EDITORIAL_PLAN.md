# My Huckleberry Life: Full-Site Editorial Plan

**Scope:** The next 90 days remain limited to the home-safety and medical-alert wedge. This plan intentionally excludes unrelated senior-lifestyle topics.

## Editorial Position

My Huckleberry Life helps adults 55+ and adult children make less-confusing choices about staying safer at home. The site should not impersonate a medical provider, a consumer laboratory, or an emergency-response service. Its near-term review content is explicitly **research-based** unless and until the founder supplies documented hands-on testing notes.

> Every recommendation needs a named buyer, a plain limitation, a traceable source, and a visible disclosure. If it cannot meet all four, it does not go live as a review.

## Review and Guide Standard

| Required element | Publishing rule |
| --- | --- |
| Review label | Label each article **Research-based guide** until documented hands-on testing is complete. Never imply it was personally tested. |
| Primary sources | Use the manufacturer’s current product page, manual, warranty/return terms, subscription terms, and privacy policy where applicable. |
| Safety context | Add a relevant government or nonprofit safety source when use, installation, falls, or emergency response are discussed. |
| Product claims | Describe only features confirmed by first-party documents. Avoid outcome claims such as “prevents falls” or “saves lives.” |
| Prices and availability | Do not place fixed prices in evergreen articles. Date-stamp any price snapshot and link to the product’s official page. |
| Limitations | Include a visible “Where this may not fit” section for every product or category. |
| Affiliate links | No affiliate link appears until a program relationship and target URL are supplied. Disclosure appears before the first commercial link and in the site footer. |
| Ratings and testimonials | Do not publish star ratings, customer-review summaries, or testimonials unless they are genuinely sourced, attributed, and permitted. Do not fabricate any user-generated content. |
| Medical and emergency caution | State that a product is not medical advice and visitors should consider their own circumstances, provider guidance, installation needs, and local emergency access. |

## Initial Editorial Portfolio

The initial portfolio focuses on the choices that move a worried adult child from “I do not know where to start” to a practical next step. Product names will be selected only after source verification.

| Article | Reader question | Required comparison fields | Core caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Medical alerts: home-only vs. on-the-go | “Does my parent need a base unit, a mobile device, or both?” | Coverage, connection type, response model, fall-detection option, charging, water resistance, cancellation terms. | A device is not a substitute for emergency planning or local response capacity. |
| Medical-alert watches | “Is a watch realistic for daily wear and charging?” | Wearability, screen/readability, charging, location sharing, mobile coverage, setup, subscription. | Alerts and fall detection can have limits, delays, false positives, or coverage dependencies. |
| Bathroom grab bars | “What is safe for this bathroom wall?” | Mounting method, wall compatibility, load guidance, finish, grip, length, installer requirements. | Suction accessories and wall-mounted bars serve different purposes; installation matters. |
| Tub transfer benches | “Can a bench reduce the need to step over the tub wall?” | Seat height, weight limit, width, reversible configuration, footprint, assembly, cleaning. | Room fit and transfer ability must be checked before purchase. |
| Bedside assist handles | “Can this help with getting in and out of bed?” | Mattress/frame fit, attachment, height range, grip, weight guidance, warnings. | Entrapment and compatibility warnings need prominent treatment. |
| Walker/rollator basics | “What should I compare before buying a rolling walker?” | Weight, fold size, height range, brake system, wheel size, transport, maintenance. | Fit and clinical guidance matter; this is not a one-size-fits-all choice. |
| Simple home safety technology | “What technology helps without turning the house into a project?” | Privacy, account setup, voice clarity, reminders, shared access, subscription, outage behavior. | Connected devices add privacy, account, and internet/power dependencies. |
| Smart smoke/CO alerts | “How can family receive an alert when they are away?” | Alarm type, battery backup, app sharing, installation, interoperability, notifications. | Follow local code and manufacturer installation guidance. |

## Page Template

Every guide or product page follows the same practical reading order: a buyer question; who it is for; what we verified; what to compare; where it may not fit; setup, subscription, and privacy cautions; an evidence ledger; and a dated update record. On a research-based product page, the lead disclosure reads: “We researched the manufacturer’s current materials and relevant safety guidance. We have not independently lab-tested this product.”

## Publication Gates

An article may advance from **planned** to **published** only when all factual claims have a current source, every commercial relationship is disclosed, all external destinations are checked, and the page meets the existing accessibility and mobile regression standards. Product pages remain without purchase buttons until the owner supplies a verified affiliate destination and confirms the relationship.
